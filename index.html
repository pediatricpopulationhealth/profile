About the Program

Science that moves across levels

The Pediatric Population Health in Action program sits within the Division of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry at Massachusetts General Hospital and spans research, clinical care, and training across Harvard Medical School. Our work spans the science of early-life adversity from the molecular level — examining epigenetic mechanisms and biological stress pathways — to the population level, tracking health outcomes across large birth cohorts and designing systems-level interventions. Our program partners closely with the Broad Trauma Initiative.

Childhood adversity and trauma are highly prevalent in the U.S. general population. Such experiences are associated with a broad range of developmental, mental, and physical health challenges — though they are not deterministic. Prevention and multilevel interventions, informed by the science of biobehavioral pathways, are central to improving population health. Our program pursues four interconnected aims:

1 Understand the impact of adversities and trauma on children's emotional, mental, and physical health.
2 Understand biobehavioral mechanisms — including epigenetic pathways and the quality of parenting — underlying childhood adversity-associated health outcomes.
3 Examine sensitive periods of development: 0–3 years and pre-puberty as critical windows of intervention opportunities to recalibrate the effects of early childhood adversities.
4 Develop and implement multilevel interventions at the individual, family, community, and systems level that bolster a child's relational health ecosystem.
5 Implement community-engaged research and clinical care that engages key stakeholders throughout design, implementation, dissemination, and program scaling.

Clinical Programs

Integrated care from screening to treatment

The Integrated Primary Care (IPC) Program
Founding Co-Directors: Archana Basu, PhD & Nancy Rotter, PhD

The IPC program embeds psychology and social work clinicians within MGH primary care pediatrics and adolescent medicine clinics. Working alongside pediatricians and adolescent medicine physicians, IPC clinicians address a full range of developmental, emotional, and behavioral concerns for children from infancy through age 21 — providing care at the point of contact where families already seek help.

Screening & Comprehensive Evaluation
Brief or detailed mental health assessments conducted during primary care appointments.
Psychoeducation & Brief Interventions
Evidence-based information, resources, and brief interventions for families navigating developmental and mental health concerns.
Curbside Consultations
Real-time consultations to primary care physicians to inform their clinical recommendations.
Triage & Resource Recommendations
Tailored plans developed collaboratively with pediatricians — including referrals for therapy, pharmacological evaluation, neuropsychological assessment, and community services.
IPC Outpatient Brief Interventions
Short-term outpatient care for focal concerns such as sleep or feeding problems, adjustment to stressors.
Acute Care Triage
Assistance triaging patients to higher levels of care including partial hospitalization and acute psychiatric services.

Intervention Spotlight

DBT-Elementary (DBT-E)

Dialectical Behavior Therapy — Elementary (DBT-E)
Lead: Makiko Watanabe, PhD · Co-Developers: Makiko Watanabe, PhD · Christina Dolce, PsyD · Archana Basu, PhD (2023)
Under Pragmatic Trial

DBT-E is a 12-week, trauma-informed, family-based intervention for children ages 7–12 with emerging emotion or behavioral dysregulation. Co-developed in 2023 from Dialectical Behavior Therapy (Linehan), DBT for Adolescents (Rathus & Miller), and Parent Management Training (Kazdin), DBT-E integrates trauma-informed care principles (SAMHSA, 2023) throughout its staff training and intervention workflows.

of children completing DBT-E had a history of at least one adverse childhood experience (ACE)
>50%

12 wk
duration — substantially briefer than traditional 6–12 month DBT programs, expanding programmatic capacity

What Makes DBT-E Distinctive

Transdiagnostic approach
Children with dysregulation symptoms — regardless of diagnosis — are well-suited to DBT-E, developed primarily for children referred by their pediatricians.
Parents as "emotion coaches"
Parents play a central role. The primary goal is to bolster parents' efficacy, teaching developmentally tailored distress tolerance skills for use during dysregulation crises.
Trauma-informed throughout
Trauma-informed care principles (SAMHSA, 2023) are integrated into staff training and all intervention workflows — recognizing the high prevalence of ACEs in this population.
Primary care partnership
Close collaboration with pediatricians enables early identification of children with emerging dysregulation and reduces wait times to access this specialized treatment.
Balance of acceptance & change
DBT-E balances acceptance and change principles and prioritizes creating a validating, nonjudgmental caregiving environment to strengthen the parent-child relationship.
Modular, skills-based design
Individual parent guidance, family therapy sessions, and skills groups give families practice with developmentally adapted DBT and behavior management skills.

Intervention Spotlight

Parenting Young Children

Parenting Young Children Group Intervention
Lead: Dana Allswede, PhD

Parenting Young Children is a 12-week, trauma-informed, group-based intervention for parents and caregivers of children ages 2–6 with emerging emotional dysregulation. Led by Dana Allswede, PhD, the intervention incorporates components of Parent-Child Interaction Therapy and Strong Roots, integrating trauma-informed care principles throughout. A Spanish-language version has also been piloted.

Developmental Guidance
Helping parents better understand their child's behavior and the developmental context of tantrums and emotional outbursts.
Behavior Navigation Strategies
Concrete, evidence-based strategies for navigating challenging behaviors including tantrums and defiance.
Parenting Pattern Awareness
Increased awareness of personal parenting patterns and how they interact with children's regulatory capacities.
Parent Coping Skills
Skills to help parents stay calm in difficult moments and co-regulate their child's feelings and behavior.
Peer Support
Connection with other parents navigating similar challenges — a validating, community-building component of the group format.
Publicly Available Resources
A library of psychoeducational materials developed by the program is freely accessible to families and clinicians.
